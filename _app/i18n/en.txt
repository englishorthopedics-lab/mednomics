§437626b EP.01 The purchasing-intermediary ban: hospitals' hidden supply channel · Mednomics
§54c9558 Two Charts · EP.01 · <span class="tag">Hospital money</span> · <time datetime="2026-10-31">Oct 31, 2026</time> · 4 min read
§e477450 The purchasing-intermediary ban: hospitals' hidden supply channel
§7d761b7 University hospitals' purchasing intermediaries are being sold one after another. Is the law closing the channel, or just changing its owner?
§d13e1b8 <a href="../">Home</a> / <a href="../series/">Series</a> / <span>The purchasing-intermediary ban: hospitals' hidden supply channel</span>
§03c57ee Key points first
§ec39d36 A purchasing intermediary (gannapsa) is a middleman that delivers medical supplies to hospitals on behalf of manufacturers.
§c0770ab The Medical Devices Act amended in December 2025 bans sales to hospitals by related-party dealers. It takes effect on Dec 31, 2027.
§64874a1 Related-party test: founder's relatives within the second degree, more than 50% ownership, or controlling influence over management.
§9f31f1f Hospitals are selling their stakes in intermediaries before it takes effect. The channel is not closing; its owners are changing.
§591da46 The way big hospitals buy supplies is changing.
§46a86f7 This year, university hospitals' purchasing intermediaries are being sold one after another.
§ddefce5 Here is why, and how it looks from the operating room.
§bb552bb <i></i>From the stock chart
§80fe470 Stents, catheters, artificial joints and surgical tools used in big hospitals' operating rooms mostly do not go straight from the manufacturer to the hospital.
§ae12a5d They pass through an indirect supplier called a gannapsa, or purchasing intermediary.
§5ca92a8 Each hospital effectively has one designated intermediary, and manufacturers or distributors must go through that door to get products in.
§20c5d88 The problem was that the main shareholder of the intermediary was often the hospital foundation or relatives of the hospital's owner.
§eb58474 So when the hospital bought supplies, people on the hospital's side took the middle margin.
§3483cba The industry put that cut at 10–20%, sometimes as much as 40%, and called it a 'toll'.
§82b67e6 Manufacturer · Importer
§40117db Distributor
§3eb5a1c Cut of 10–20%, up to 40%
§48118fd Hospital · Operating room
§39f58fc People on the hospital side were often the intermediary's main shareholders
§e761a80 How surgical supplies reach the operating room. Graphic · Mednomics
§a1680b9 The amended Medical Devices Act, passed by the National Assembly in December 2025, blocks this structure.
§a3e621f Device dealers may not sell to hospitals with which they have a 'related-party' relationship. Routing sales through another company to get around it is also banned.
§d9f2cd4 There are three related-party tests: relatives of the hospital founder within the second degree, ownership of more than 50%, or controlling influence over management regardless of ownership.
§6d61fe6 Violations can lead to criminal penalties. The law takes effect on Dec 31, 2027.
§ae24014 So hospitals are selling their stakes in intermediaries before then.
§7cc2b44 Severance Hospital's intermediary, Avison Care, went in May to Ezmedicom, which is controlled by Daewoong Pharmaceutical's owner family.
§93b7291 Two intermediaries linked to the Catholic Medical Center went in July to Serveone, a private-equity-owned company.
§a2a24f4 Asan Medical Center has also put 51% of its intermediary, Medigood Partners, up for sale.
§44b5ff4 One number captures the structure. Avison Care reportedly made KRW 21.6bn in operating profit on KRW 22.9bn of revenue last fiscal year.
§66e459a <span class="hl">A company that keeps more than 90% of its revenue as profit is less a logistics firm than a fee window.</span>
§d19aa70 <i></i>From the patient chart
§1d75cf0 Surgeons choose which materials to use, but rarely know what they cost or whose hands they pass through.
§eb346fc All a doctor sees is the hospital's approved materials list and what is laid out on the day of surgery.
§e4a7e39 Even if you want to try a new instrument, it cannot reach the operating room unless it is on the hospital's purchasing list and the intermediary's route.
§4e14d6b So for doctors, the intermediary problem is a problem of choice before it is a problem of price.
§2cdf60b When the middle cut is large, big multinationals can absorb it, but small Korean manufacturers and distributors with thin margins give out first.
§b0e9238 Even more so when payment is delayed by months. That is why the amended law also requires payment within six months, with interest on late payment.
§ef2c609 News reports name only university hospitals, but this is not just their story.
§b69a2d2 At the spine and joint specialty hospitals I have seen, the intermediary was often a company run by an acquaintance on the hospital's side.
§a37809e But the law targets relatives, ownership and control. <span class="hl">A company owned and run separately by a close acquaintance may not be covered on paper.</span>
§fb6c088 <i></i>Overlaying the two charts
§b791375 The law does not abolish the intermediary channel. If the hospital side cuts its stake below 50%, business can continue.
§c3886a6 <span class="hl">What is happening now is not the closing of the channel but a change of owner.</span>
§39394fa Hospital foundations cash out by selling their stakes; private equity and large distributors gain university hospitals as reliable customers.
§81ccc5a On this trend, some 80 intermediaries nationwide are likely to consolidate into a few large operators.
§fd2cc12 If they become companies that earn money from purchasing, logistics and inventory services, like US group purchasing organizations (GPOs), both hospitals and manufacturers could gain.
§79d80ed On the other hand, bigger intermediaries become more powerful buyers in dealings with manufacturers.
§1ba402c People in the field also point out that the rules could be dodged by adjusting stakes or by hospitals using each other's intermediaries.
§0384650 What to watch
§04934d6 Who ends up with Asan Medical Center's Medigood Partners
§7ddaddd Whether larger intermediaries add logistics and inventory services or simply keep collecting fees
§af1aeef How well the enforcement decree and the health ministry's audits stop workaround deals
§8c4c572 Whether small Korean device makers actually get paid faster
§2b87b89 In one line
§f2b34c3 The law changes the channel's owner rather than abolishing it. Whether the 'toll' is gone will show not in the news but in whether the shelves in the operating room carry a wider range of supplies.
§ae2ce92 Frequently asked questions
§868623f What is a purchasing intermediary (gannapsa)?
§e70a8d6 An indirect supplier that receives medical supplies from manufacturers or distributors and delivers them to hospitals on their behalf. Many hospitals effectively had one designated intermediary.
§eb9f557 When does the intermediary ban take effect?
§c00c19e The amended Medical Devices Act, passed in December 2025, takes effect on Dec 31, 2027.
§c640c4f Will intermediaries disappear once the law takes effect?
§6c09e46 No. The law blocks related-party deals, such as those involving the founder's relatives or more than 50% ownership. If the hospital side lowers its stake, business can continue, so what is happening now is a change of owner rather than the closing of the channel.

§1265c2d The classic case is Kangdong Sacred Heart (Sungsim) Hospital and Samchundang Pharm.
§8e86388 <i></i>Roots: hospitals founded by one doctor
§fb054c1 The Sungsim group traces back to surgeon Dr. Yoon Duk-sun, who set up the Sungsim Medical Foundation in 1974 and the Ilsong educational foundation in 1982.
§e6ddcb5 He founded Kangnam, Chuncheon and Kangdong Sacred Heart hospitals in turn and is regarded as the doctor who founded the most hospitals in Korea.
§fef31a3 The hospitals were passed down along two lines.
§0029247 The eldest son, Yoon Dae-won, ran the Ilsong foundation from 1989, leading the Sacred Heart hospitals of the Hallym University Medical Center. He died in 2024.
§9f41ce5 The second son, Chairman Yoon Dae-in, inherited the Sungsim Medical Foundation and Kangdong Sacred Heart Hospital.
§33deada In 1986 he <span class="hl">bought Samchundang Pharm and built a business that supplies drugs to the foundation's hospitals.</span>
§5e53efe <i></i>Trunk: a drugmaker grown beside the hospital
§9bb2ce6 At the center is an unlisted company called Soha.
§f134c77 Soha was reported in the early 2000s as a supplier of drugs to the Hallym University Medical Center. Its 2004 revenue was KRW 88.1bn.
§65a3a45 Soha was also Samchundang's largest shareholder with 42.5%, and Soha's largest shareholder was Chairman Yoon Dae-in.
§3c7f191 <span class="hl">People on the hospital side owned the company that supplied the drugs, and that company in turn owned a drugmaker.</span>
§6ac974b The skeleton is the same today. Soha is Samchundang's largest shareholder (about 31%); Soha is owned 56.52% by Chairman Yoon Dae-in and 43.48% by Insan MTS, a company wholly owned by his eldest son, Yoon Hee-je.
§d5076b0 Analysts say management sits with CEO Jeon In-seok, the son-in-law, while control rests with father and son.
§2205f45 A market built on reliable, captive customers is called a 'captive market'. Critics have called it funneling business to affiliates.
§5063a5a <i></i>The 2026 stock chart
§33e8c41 This company shook the market this year.
§4bea59c The share price went from about KRW 230,000 at the end of 2025 to over KRW 1m in three months, making it KOSDAQ's largest company with a KRW 26tn market cap.
§343eb1a The catalyst was hope around oral insulin (the S-PASS platform) and generic deals for oral diabetes and obesity drugs.
§21b2cd2 Yet 2025 revenue was KRW 231.8bn and operating profit KRW 8.5bn. <span class="hl">A company with under KRW 10bn in operating profit ranked around 30th by market cap among all listed companies.</span>
§d204dd6 After a US deal was announced in late March without naming the partner, doubts grew and the share price halved within three days.
§e533b27 The exchange designated it an unfaithful-disclosure company for releasing earnings guidance only in a press release, not a filing (5 penalty points).
§834e6fb Around the same time, the former CEO planned and then withdrew a KRW 250bn share sale to pay tax on shares gifted by his father-in-law.
§d1290d8 To be used in a big hospital, a drug must first pass an internal committee and be added to the hospital formulary.
§5ddc502 Once on the formulary, prescriptions come steadily, and drugs started during a stay tend to continue as outpatient prescriptions after discharge.
§3a71aeb So for drugmakers, a big hospital is not just a customer but the first gate into the market.
§0bd9a0b <span class="hl">If a company from the same family stands beside that gate, it starts from a different line.</span>
§0954828 Still, today's share price cannot be credited to the hospital. What the market is buying is not hospital supply revenue but a future built on oral insulin.
§a0a53d5 Last episode's intermediary ban comes up again here.
§76cc505 The amended Medical Devices Act blocks deals with related-party dealers, such as the founder's relatives within the second degree or holders of more than 50%. The Pharmaceutical Affairs Act already has a similar rule for drugs.
§5b3793c The person who runs a hospital foundation, a distributor owned by him and his son, and a drugmaker whose largest shareholder is that distributor. <span class="hl">On structure alone, it resembles what the amended law targets.</span>
§946900b Whether the law actually applies depends on what each company sells to which hospital and how the enforcement decree defines related parties. It is not something to state as fact.
§547b3ef Still, with the law taking effect at the end of 2027, how the group reorganizes the ownership and dealings of Soha and Insan MTS is worth watching.

§a89d7fe In two years MBK bought implants, intraoral scanners, drug distribution and a Japanese energy-tonic maker. What they share is demand that does not switch off when people are sick, old or tired.
§3de77c6 <a href="../">Home</a> / <a href="../series/">Series</a> / <span>Why private equity bought drug distribution and dental implants</span>
§8565d69 MBK bought Medit (signed Dec 2022, about KRW 2.4tn), Osstem Implant (2023, about KRW 2.5tn), Geo-Young (Apr 2024, 71.25% of the holding company for KRW 1.95tn) and Alinamin Pharmaceutical (Jul 2024, JPY 350bn).
§ff53cb5 Geo-Young deals with about 80% of Korea's pharmacies, yet its 2025 consolidated operating margin was under 2% (revenue KRW 5.35tn, operating profit KRW 103.6bn).
§50a0f06 Geo-Young's holding company carried out a KRW 274.6bn paid capital reduction in July 2024, and reports say its debt ratio jumped from 506% to 1,600%.
§a6231ce Care Camp, Samsung Medical Center's main purchasing intermediary, is a Geo-Young affiliate.
§b35642a The last two episodes followed money from the hospital side.
§0969689 This time we look at the other side: the capital buying into healthcare.
§22e728c We follow the companies bought over two years by MBK Partners, Korea's largest private equity firm.
§fccfa9c <i></i>From the stock chart: what it bought
§983390a In 2023 MBK, together with Unison Capital Korea (UCK), acquired Osstem Implant, Korea's No. 1 implant company.
§84b6aaf It was shaken at the time by an employee's embezzlement of more than KRW 200bn. Part of the founder's stake and minority shares were bought at the same price (KRW 190,000 a share) through a tender offer.
§522e943 Two tender offers took the stake above 96%, and the company was delisted in August 2023. The deal was reported at about KRW 2.5tn.
§4781576 Earlier, in December 2022, MBK signed to buy 99.5% of intraoral scanner maker Medit for about KRW 2.4tn, putting implants and digital dental equipment in one hand.
§38eed10 In April 2024 it bought Geo-Young, Korea's largest drug distributor, from Blackstone: 71.25% of the holding company for KRW 1.95tn, valuing the whole at about KRW 2tn.
§7b12a68 Blackstone had bought Geo-Young in 2019 for about KRW 1.1tn. <span class="hl">Its price nearly doubled in five years.</span>
§e72e770 In July that year it bought Japan's Alinamin Pharmaceutical, also from Blackstone, for JPY 350bn (about KRW 2.9tn). It makes Alinamin, Japan's best-known energy tonic, and was once Takeda's consumer health unit.

§2ebfe63 <i></i>Why it bought: the private equity math
§5230957 Private equity buys companies with other people's money and borrowed money, then sells them for more a few years later.
§4365b8f To repay the borrowing, it needs cash that comes in reliably every year.
§8372490 Healthcare fits that well. <span class="hl">In a downturn people still need medicine, missing teeth still need replacing, and tired people still buy vitamins.</span>
§14d9759 Indeed, MBK raised KRW 900bn in acquisition financing to buy Geo-Young, a structure made possible by steady cash flow.
§1f2fe79 Second is the 'channel'. Geo-Young deals with about 80% of pharmacies nationwide: it owns the highway that drugs travel on.
§4341bcf But distribution margins are thin. <span class="hl">Geo-Young's 2025 consolidated operating margin was under 2%:</span> KRW 103.6bn of operating profit on KRW 5.35tn of revenue.
§82ff411 Third is bundling. Owning both an implant maker and a scanner maker means more products to sell to each dental clinic. The industry calls this a 'bolt-on' strategy.
§06e9307 Once doctors get used to a device, they rarely switch.
§68c956c Artificial joints in orthopedics, too, differ in instruments and steps by company, so surgeons tend to stick with the system they first learned.
§9759f87 That is why device companies invest heavily in training doctors. Osstem Implant's growth in clinical education can be seen the same way.
§dff14ff <span class="hl">A doctor who is used to a system does not leave easily. To private equity, that looks like predictable revenue.</span>
§f642e7b Drug distribution is almost invisible to doctors most of the time. It shows when drugs run out, and that is when the power of whoever holds the network becomes clear.
§de42965 And there is a link to last time: Care Camp, Samsung Medical Center's main purchasing intermediary, is a Geo-Young affiliate.
§bee44b6 With university hospitals' intermediaries flooding the market, a distributor that already handles hospital logistics is well placed.
§27e867f The private equity formula is usually similar: buy with debt, raise efficiency, take some money back along the way, sell for more.
§b3eb4ea Right after the deal, in July 2024, Geo-Young's holding company carried out a KRW 274.6bn paid capital reduction. Observers said MBK recovered about KRW 200bn in the process.
§0ccce7a Over the same period, reports said the holding company's debt ratio jumped from 506% at the end of 2023 to 1,600% at the end of 2024, drawing criticism that its finances had worsened.
§31ea7a5 The same formula has also failed. Homeplus, which MBK bought with heavy debt, entered court receivership in 2025.
§d8f8f46 The difference is the nature of demand. Supermarkets swing with competition and the economy; medicine and treatment do not shrink easily. That is why healthcare appeals to private equity.
§e4b7a11 But <span class="hl">at the end of this cash flow is the patient.</span> If cost-cutting goes too far and drug supply wobbles or device prices rise, the burden falls on patients and national insurance.

§a4e811b Private equity buys healthcare for a simple reason: sick people buy medicine, missing teeth get replaced, tired people take vitamins. Debt can be stacked on demand that never switches off. What sets it apart from other industries is that the owners of that demand are patients.
§b36c66e What did MBK Partners buy in healthcare?
§349dd7a Dental intraoral scanner maker Medit, No. 1 implant maker Osstem Implant, No. 1 drug distributor Geo-Young, and Japan's Alinamin Pharmaceutical.
§da429dd Why does private equity like healthcare?
§4fd5ec4 Cash comes in steadily every year regardless of the economy, which makes debt easier to repay, and there are barriers to entry such as drug distribution networks and doctors' device habits.
§e961494 What happened after the Geo-Young deal?
§5a7f688 Right after the deal, in July 2024, the holding company carried out a KRW 274.6bn paid capital reduction, and reports said its debt ratio rose sharply.

§a9ed8d4 <a href="../">Home</a> / <a href="../series/">Series</a> / <span>The obesity drug three-way race and the economics of knees: Wegovy, Mounjaro, efpeglenatide</span>

§32d71fb Korea's first home-grown obesity drug, efpeglenatide (Hanmi Pharmaceutical), was approved on Oct 7, 2026, with supply starting Oct 29.
§a950eee Wegovy applied for national insurance coverage on Oct 12, and the government is reviewing 'managed coverage' for obesity drugs.

§0469a28 Leadership of Korea's obesity drug market flipped within a year.
§850978c Efpeglenatide, the first Korean-made obesity drug, also started being prescribed this week.
§e0c02b8 The numbers are already in. The knee is still a hypothesis. We look at them separately.
§cfaa555 <i></i>From the stock chart: market numbers
§11f2220 Wegovy launched in Korea in October 2024 and Mounjaro in August 2025.
§ea9ecde Data from IQVIA put first-half 2026 sales at about KRW 744bn for Mounjaro and KRW 217bn for Wegovy, more than KRW 960bn combined.
§66c3f5e By quarter: Q1 Mounjaro KRW 323.2bn vs Wegovy KRW 104bn; Q2 KRW 421.2bn vs KRW 113bn. The gap widened to about 3.7 times.

§e608e19 <span class="hl">Mounjaro alone sold more than twice as much in Q2 as the cancer drug Keytruda (KRW 149.8bn).</span> It is the No. 1 drug in Korea.
§1832992 Wegovy cut its supply price by about 40% when Mounjaro arrived. Even so, Q2 sales fell 18.4% from a year earlier, though they rose 8.6% from the previous quarter, ending two straight quarters of decline.
§f0dc5e3 On Oct 7, Hanmi Pharmaceutical's efpeglenatide was approved by the drug safety ministry, the 45th new drug developed in Korea and the first Korean obesity drug. Supply began on Oct 29.
§6627a8c Efpeglenatide's first-month price is KRW 129,800 including VAT, rising to KRW 299,200 from the fourth month. Wegovy and Mounjaro were reported at over KRW 200,000 for four weeks of the lowest dose.
§dc28ab4 In the Korean Phase 3 trial (448 people, 40-week interim results), efpeglenatide cut weight by 9.75% on average versus 0.95% for placebo; 79.4% lost at least 5% and 49.5% at least 10%.
§84be2fe But this is a 40-week interim analysis of a 64-week trial, while Wegovy and Mounjaro data are from 68–72 weeks, and there is no head-to-head trial. <span class="hl">Current data cannot rank the three drugs.</span>
§1a1ba5c On Oct 12, Novo Nordisk applied for insurance coverage of Wegovy. At the Oct 28 parliamentary audit, Health Minister Jeong Eun-kyeong said the government is reviewing putting obesity drugs such as Wegovy and Mounjaro under 'managed coverage'.
§4c62f01 Eligibility, the obesity threshold and the co-pay have not been set. <span class="hl">The coming question is not price but who pays how much.</span>
§7914b3a <i></i>From the patient chart: the knee
§bc467c0 The most common advice for knee arthritis patients is 'lose some weight'.
§d8e0069 More than 3 million people a year are treated for knee arthritis (2024, NHIS data). Total knee replacements reached 78,300 in 2025, up 5.2% from 2021.
§853fa7e More than 80% of artificial knee joints used are imported: 82.6% in 2025.
§b795140 That is why the STEP 9 trial, published in NEJM in October 2024, drew attention. It tested whether an obesity drug also helps knee pain.
§68eaf0d It enrolled 407 people with a BMI of 30 or more, moderate knee osteoarthritis and at least moderate pain, randomized 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks.
§5ebdd3a Weight fell 13.7% versus 3.2%, and pain scores (WOMAC, 0–100) fell 41.7 points versus 27.5. Physical function scores improved about twice as much.
§38ceabb Three cautions in reading the numbers.
§ce23a3f <span class="hl">First, pain also fell 27.5 points in the placebo group.</span> Diet and exercise counseling alone had a big effect; the drug added about 14 points.
§331f2db Second, the average BMI was 40.3, 81.6% were women and the average age was 56. That is a different group from the typical knee patient in a Korean outpatient clinic.
§5597754 Third, 6.7% stopped treatment due to side effects, more than double the 3.0% on placebo, mostly gastrointestinal. There is no long-term data yet.
§a408a29 An earlier trial of liraglutide 3.0 mg, from the same drug class, showed no difference in pain. Not every drug in this class is the same.
§1755d1e Semaglutide is approved for obesity and diabetes, not as an osteoarthritis treatment.
§9e3dff4 In March, Cell Metabolism published a mouse study showing changes in cartilage cell metabolism even without weight loss. In an exploratory study of 20 people, MRI after 24 weeks showed medial knee cartilage about 17% thicker on average.
§f7f7fdc The researchers themselves urged caution given the small size. <span class="hl">It is a hypothesis.</span>
§10fae54 There is one question: will obesity drugs reduce knee surgery?
§9f99162 The numbers cannot answer it yet. All we can confirm is that the five-year trend in knee replacements is still rising after obesity drugs arrived.
§74f45c4 The hypotheses cut both ways. Less pain before surgery could delay operations. Or severely obese patients who could not be operated on because of their weight could become candidates.
§5d405fa Either way, with more than 80% of artificial joints imported, foreign manufacturers will feel any change in demand first.
§c0cc98c The cost structures also differ. Obesity drugs are now uninsured and cost hundreds of thousands of won a month, while surgery is covered by insurance. If the drugs are covered, the question becomes whether the insurance fund can afford it.
§9a8497a One thing is clear. When drugs take weight off, muscle tends to go too. Experts recommend weight management together with thigh-strengthening exercise for the knees.

§c9e8553 But the direction is clear. Aesthetic medicine, once centered on cosmetic surgery, is shifting to non-surgical procedures such as Botox, fillers, lasers and lifting, which the press calls 'petite' cosmetic procedures.
§1ab058f The nature of demand has changed too. Double-eyelid and nose surgery is one-off, but skin care is repeated every few months. Analysts say regular care by middle-aged customers supports the market even as the population in their 20s and 30s shrinks.
§80bfdf4 Most of these procedures are uninsured, so clinics set the prices. High patient turnover and repeat bookings make revenue easy to predict. That is what draws doctors.
§6b9a409 There are dispute numbers too. Last year the Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency received 175 mediation or arbitration requests involving plastic surgery and 114 involving dermatology. Surgery carries the burden that an accident can mean permanent disability and large damages.
§c850892 <i></i>From the patient chart: foreign patients
§cbcf798 Health ministry data show 1.313 million foreign patients received dermatology care in Korea last year, 62.9% of all visits by specialty. Plastic surgery drew 233,000 (11.2%); dermatology was more than five times larger.

§fc21d4f Many dermatologists I know opened in Apgujeong and see foreign patients. That points the same way as the numbers above.
§452f85b Plastic surgery has not disappeared. A plastic surgeon I know told me his main patients are celebrities from Thailand and elsewhere in Southeast Asia. Where foreign demand is strong, there is still room for cosmetic surgery.
§e87074e There are 3,319 solo clinics in Seoul registered with dermatology as a specialty offered. HIRA data show only 385 (11.6%) have a board-certified dermatologist. The rest are run by general practitioners, family physicians, plastic surgeons and other specialists.
§90582d4 Becoming a board-certified plastic surgeon takes at least five years of surgical training: one year of internship and four of residency. The core of the reports is that more people are leaving those five years behind for procedure-based practice. One clinic in Seocho-dong was reported to have four plastic surgeons working as directors.
§5ce05fa <i></i>Overlaying the two charts: hospital on-call duty
§66d9f82 General hospitals often say it is hard to find on-call doctors these days, and the reason frequently given is that doctors have moved into skin and aesthetics.
§7916ae3 <span class="hl">That part is not proven by these reports and statistics.</span> We could not find official data directly linking the number of doctors who moved to dermatology with gaps in hospital on-call cover. It is a perception in the field and a hypothesis.
§d23ca54 The logic is understandable, though. On-call work means nights awake, while procedure-based outpatient work ends in the daytime and runs on bookings. With two options under the same license, doctors compare both time and income.
§711631d When hospitals cannot find staff, they must raise on-call pay or cut night services. That cost lands on hospital profitability and on capacity for emergency and night care.
§3688c45 The debate over doctors flowing from essential care into aesthetics is long-running. But it is tangled up with medical school quotas, fees and working conditions, so no single number settles it.
§ae2b158 One thing remains for patients: most clinics with a dermatology sign do not have a board-certified dermatologist. You can check which specialty a doctor is certified in before a procedure.

§7301272 About 77.5% of a drug wholesaler's shares (57 people) were held by spouses and children of directors of 15 client hospitals. Since 2013 it paid out 49.6% of net profit on average.
§99d744d The tax office treated the dividends as rebates received by nine doctors (other income) and issued a notice of income reclassification.
§999685c The Tax Tribunal (2025-Seo-3143, late October 2026) overturned it in full. Dividends ranged from KRW 4 to KRW 148 per KRW 100 of drug purchases, not in proportion to prescriptions, and 58% of revenue came from ordinary customers.
§1c15cbc The ruling concerns the tax assessment only, not whether the Medical Service Act or the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act was broken. In 2025, rebates paid as dividends through a shell company led to criminal charges.
§ef4f1d4 There was a drug wholesaler whose shares were more than three-quarters owned by spouses and children of directors of its client hospitals.
§7c24ff8 The tax office saw the dividends as payment for using its drugs, in other words rebates. The Tax Tribunal ruled the opposite.
§9e96122 We separate how the two bodies read the same numbers so differently.
§d94c7d8 <i></i>From the stock chart: a wholesaler that pays big dividends
§4880918 It is a wholesaler that supplies prescription drugs and medical devices to hospitals and pharmacies. The CEO started it as a sole proprietorship in 2001 and incorporated it in 2007.
§9f0822c From 2010, the CEO began selling shares to client hospital directors and their families. By the end of that year his side held only 20%; the other 80% had gone to the directors' side.
§649bbe3 Revenue roughly tripled the following year and kept growing after that.
§b7d61a1 At the end of 2022, the shareholder register listed 57 spouses and children of client doctors, holding about 77.5% between them and linked to 15 hospitals. The company has more than 400 customers in all.
§9321d73 Since 2013 the company paid out 49.6% of net profit on average every year, reportedly two to three times the average for listed companies.
§7e678c0 The case begins with <span class="hl">a company that returned half of what it earned to shareholders every year</span>, most of whom were family members of client hospital directors.
§e086da3 <i></i>From the clinic: between prescriptions and dividends
§aec9e59 In August 2024 the Seoul Regional Tax Office audited the company and found a profit-and-loss file that broke out profit by hospital for just the 15 hospitals and their nearby pharmacies.
§62dc968 The structure the tax office drew was simple: doctors prescribe, the company earns, and part of the earnings goes to doctors' families as 'dividends'.
§94fee6d The Medical Service Act and the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act ban both giving and receiving money in return for using drugs. So the tax office concluded that families had been made shareholders and the payments wrapped as dividends.
§c478107 Its evidence was timing: families bought shares around when a hospital started doing business and sold them before the register closed in the year after the business ended.
§744ab44 It also pointed to stake adjustments. One hospital that brought the company less profit saw its stake cut from 5% to 2.5%. By the tax office's math, 20–50% of the profit generated by prescriptions in 2021 went back as dividends.
§31df504 Other evidence included a shareholder's statement that the CEO introduced buyers and sellers, and share prices below the tax-law valuation. In 2023 a second company with a similar structure was set up, with minor children of client directors among its shareholders.
§6526d49 The tax office treated the dividends as rebates received by nine doctors, classed as other income for tax purposes, and issued a notice of income reclassification ordering the company to withhold tax.
§bf99d7d <i></i>Overlaying the two charts: dividends followed share counts
§8bbb8d4 The company's rebuttal started with arithmetic. If these were rebates, hospitals that used more drugs should get more and those that used fewer should get less. At the very least, you cannot give back more than the drugs you sold.
§de5cf79 The actual dividends did not fit. Taking a hospital's drug purchases as KRW 100, one director's family received KRW 4 in dividends and another KRW 148. <span class="hl">Some received more in dividends than the hospital spent on drugs.</span>

§a14a2f8 Dividends are paid by the number of shares held, not by prescriptions. So the company argued this money was dividends, not rebates.
§3178cc3 Shareholders said they bought shares with their own money and paid gift tax where the money came from parents or spouses. Some hospitals' families bought shares as long as five years and ten months after business began.
§668423b The company also argued that profit analysis by customer is something every wholesaler does, and noted that the tax office changed its legal reasoning between the objection and the appeal stages.
§0fea232 In late October 2026, the Tax Tribunal (2025-Seo-3143) overturned the tax office's notice in full.
§32c2584 The tribunal's test: to treat a transaction as a sham, the tax office must prove it was arranged to avoid tax. A rebate requires a visible causal link, 'paid because they prescribed', but the dividends were paid by share count regardless of prescriptions or sales.
§68f30a6 58% of the company's revenue and gross profit came from ordinary customers, not hospitals or pharmacies linked to shareholders. Nor was there evidence that profit had been channeled to particular shareholders.
§9492e25 So it ruled that <span class="hl">labeling the payments rebates simply because the shareholders were doctors' families stretched the substance-over-form principle too far.</span>
§7a12916 <i></i>Same dividends, different conclusions
§f59b11f This ruling is about whether the tax assessment was right. It did not decide whether the Medical Service Act or the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act was broken. Criminal proceedings run separately.
§fcd5859 Under the dual-punishment rule introduced in 2010, doctors who receive rebates are punished as well as those who give them. Recipients face up to three years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30m, and license suspension.
§2817781 Dividends have actually been prosecuted as rebates. In August 2025 the Seoul Western District Prosecutors' Office indicted a wholesaler that set up a shell company, gave shares to the family of a general hospital's chair and passed about KRW 5bn through dividends and other payments: KRW 3.4bn as dividends and about KRW 1.6bn as fake salaries, corporate cards and the like.
§9b16e2e The difference between the two cases is the substance of the company and the flow of money. The Western District case involved a shell set up to pay rebates, plus bid rigging. This company does real business with more than 400 customers, and over half its profit came from sources unrelated to shareholders.
§5434c14 The Pharmaceutical Affairs Act bars a wholesaler effectively controlled by a hospital founder or related party from selling drugs to that hospital. The rule touches the hospital–distributor structure in EP.02. Here, families from 15 hospitals split the shares, so no single hospital controlled the company.
§e23a56d <span class="hl">The tribunal did not say this structure is fine.</span> It means that tying client hospital directors' families in as shareholders was not proven to be a rebate by tax standards.
§2a36189 The rebate debate never ends because, critics say, the cost ends up in drug prices and the insurance fund. Whether the same money is a dividend or a payment will have to be argued in numbers, case by case.
