    Starting the series: why two charts

    Patient charts and stock charts are more alike than you might think. Both are records of the past used to judge what comes next.

    Home / Series / Starting the series: why two charts

    A few notes to start the series.

    The patient chart
    01

    A doctor's day starts with reading charts.

    02

    Yesterday's vital signs, last week's lab results, a surgical record from months ago: they decide what to do today.

    03

    No doctor judges from a single number. The same value means different things depending on the course the patient has followed.

    The stock chart
    04

    People who read stock charts do something similar, weaving past prices, earnings and filings together to gauge what lies ahead.

    05

    Yet in healthcare, the two charts rarely meet.

    06

    Investors read news of drug approvals, device clearances and hospital deals as numbers.

    07

    But how fast a drug is prescribed in the clinic, or how an instrument is judged in the operating room, rarely makes the news.

    08

    Doctors know the front line better than anyone but tend not to notice the money flows it stands on.

    09

    They seldom ask how the supplies they use every day arrive, or who owns the company that makes the drugs they prescribe.

    Two charts
    10

    Read only one chart and you see only half.

    11

    This series tries to lay the two charts side by side.

    12

    Each episode sums up public news and filings and adds what looks different from the patient-chart side.

    13

    Some principles too. I use public sources only. I do not use undisclosed information heard from anyone.

    14

    I do not tell you to buy or sell any stock. If I gain an interest in a company I cover, I say so. I do not write anything that could identify a patient.

    In one line

    For investors, I want to show the front line behind the numbers; for people in medicine, the money behind the front line. The first episode is about purchasing intermediaries, the channel hospitals buy through.

    Dr. Jordan
