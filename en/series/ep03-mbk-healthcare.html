    Why private equity bought drug distribution and dental implants

    In two years MBK bought implants, intraoral scanners, drug distribution and a Japanese energy-tonic maker. What they share is demand that does not switch off when people are sick, old or tired.

    Home / Series / Why private equity bought drug distribution and dental implants

    Key points first

    • MBK bought Medit (signed Dec 2022, about KRW 2.4tn), Osstem Implant (2023, about KRW 2.5tn), Geo-Young (Apr 2024, 71.25% of the holding company for KRW 1.95tn) and Alinamin Pharmaceutical (Jul 2024, JPY 350bn).
    • Geo-Young deals with about 80% of Korea's pharmacies, yet its 2025 consolidated operating margin was under 2% (revenue KRW 5.35tn, operating profit KRW 103.6bn).
    • Geo-Young's holding company carried out a KRW 274.6bn paid capital reduction in July 2024, and reports say its debt ratio jumped from 506% to 1,600%.
    • Care Camp, Samsung Medical Center's main purchasing intermediary, is a Geo-Young affiliate.

    The last two episodes followed money from the hospital side.

    This time we look at the other side: the capital buying into healthcare.

    We follow the companies bought over two years by MBK Partners, Korea's largest private equity firm.

    From the stock chart: what it bought
    01

    In 2023 MBK, together with Unison Capital Korea (UCK), acquired Osstem Implant, Korea's No. 1 implant company.

    02

    It was shaken at the time by an employee's embezzlement of more than KRW 200bn. Part of the founder's stake and minority shares were bought at the same price (KRW 190,000 a share) through a tender offer.

    03

    Two tender offers took the stake above 96%, and the company was delisted in August 2023. The deal was reported at about KRW 2.5tn.

    04

    Earlier, in December 2022, MBK signed to buy 99.5% of intraoral scanner maker Medit for about KRW 2.4tn, putting implants and digital dental equipment in one hand.

    05

    In April 2024 it bought Geo-Young, Korea's largest drug distributor, from Blackstone: 71.25% of the holding company for KRW 1.95tn, valuing the whole at about KRW 2tn.

    06

    Blackstone had bought Geo-Young in 2019 for about KRW 1.1tn. Its price nearly doubled in five years.

    07

    In July that year it bought Japan's Alinamin Pharmaceutical, also from Blackstone, for JPY 350bn (about KRW 2.9tn). It makes Alinamin, Japan's best-known energy tonic, and was once Takeda's consumer health unit.

    Why it bought: the private equity math
    08

    Private equity buys companies with other people's money and borrowed money, then sells them for more a few years later.

    09

    To repay the borrowing, it needs cash that comes in reliably every year.

    10

    Healthcare fits that well. In a downturn people still need medicine, missing teeth still need replacing, and tired people still buy vitamins.

    11

    Indeed, MBK raised KRW 900bn in acquisition financing to buy Geo-Young, a structure made possible by steady cash flow.

    12

    Second is the 'channel'. Geo-Young deals with about 80% of pharmacies nationwide: it owns the highway that drugs travel on.

    13

    But distribution margins are thin. Geo-Young's 2025 consolidated operating margin was under 2%: KRW 103.6bn of operating profit on KRW 5.35tn of revenue.

    14

    Third is bundling. Owning both an implant maker and a scanner maker means more products to sell to each dental clinic. The industry calls this a 'bolt-on' strategy.

    From the patient chart
    15

    Once doctors get used to a device, they rarely switch.

    16

    Artificial joints in orthopedics, too, differ in instruments and steps by company, so surgeons tend to stick with the system they first learned.

    17

    That is why device companies invest heavily in training doctors. Osstem Implant's growth in clinical education can be seen the same way.

    18

    A doctor who is used to a system does not leave easily. To private equity, that looks like predictable revenue.

    19

    Drug distribution is almost invisible to doctors most of the time. It shows when drugs run out, and that is when the power of whoever holds the network becomes clear.

    20

    And there is a link to last time: Care Camp, Samsung Medical Center's main purchasing intermediary, is a Geo-Young affiliate.

    21

    With university hospitals' intermediaries flooding the market, a distributor that already handles hospital logistics is well placed.

    Overlaying the two charts
    22

    The private equity formula is usually similar: buy with debt, raise efficiency, take some money back along the way, sell for more.

    23

    Right after the deal, in July 2024, Geo-Young's holding company carried out a KRW 274.6bn paid capital reduction. Observers said MBK recovered about KRW 200bn in the process.

    24

    Over the same period, reports said the holding company's debt ratio jumped from 506% at the end of 2023 to 1,600% at the end of 2024, drawing criticism that its finances had worsened.

    25

    The same formula has also failed. Homeplus, which MBK bought with heavy debt, entered court receivership in 2025.

    26

    The difference is the nature of demand. Supermarkets swing with competition and the economy; medicine and treatment do not shrink easily. That is why healthcare appeals to private equity.

    27

    But at the end of this cash flow is the patient. If cost-cutting goes too far and drug supply wobbles or device prices rise, the burden falls on patients and national insurance.

    What to watch

    1. Whether Geo-Young buys any of the university hospital intermediaries for sale
    2. When, for how much and how (relisting or sale) MBK exits Osstem Implant and Medit
    3. Whether Alinamin Pharmaceutical expands into Korea and Asia
    4. How MBK's financing and reputation after Homeplus affect its other healthcare investments
    In one line

    Private equity buys healthcare for a simple reason: sick people buy medicine, missing teeth get replaced, tired people take vitamins. Debt can be stacked on demand that never switches off. What sets it apart from other industries is that the owners of that demand are patients.

    Frequently asked questions

    What did MBK Partners buy in healthcare?

    Dental intraoral scanner maker Medit, No. 1 implant maker Osstem Implant, No. 1 drug distributor Geo-Young, and Japan's Alinamin Pharmaceutical.

    Why does private equity like healthcare?

    Cash comes in steadily every year regardless of the economy, which makes debt easier to repay, and there are barriers to entry such as drug distribution networks and doctors' device habits.

    What happened after the Geo-Young deal?

    Right after the deal, in July 2024, the holding company carried out a KRW 274.6bn paid capital reduction, and reports said its debt ratio rose sharply.
