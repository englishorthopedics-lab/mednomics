    The obesity drug three-way race and the economics of knees: Wegovy, Mounjaro, efpeglenatide

    Mounjaro overtook Wegovy and Korea's own efpeglenatide has joined. Nobody yet knows whether obesity drugs will shake the knee surgery market.

    Home / Series / The obesity drug three-way race and the economics of knees: Wegovy, Mounjaro, efpeglenatide

    Key points first

    • IQVIA data for Q2 2026: Mounjaro KRW 421.2bn, Wegovy KRW 113bn. A gap of about 3.7 times.
    • Korea's first home-grown obesity drug, efpeglenatide (Hanmi Pharmaceutical), was approved on Oct 7, 2026, with supply starting Oct 29.
    • Wegovy applied for national insurance coverage on Oct 12, and the government is reviewing 'managed coverage' for obesity drugs.
    • STEP 9 (NEJM 2024): 68 weeks of semaglutide cut pain scores by 41.7 points versus 27.5. The placebo group also improved a lot.

    Leadership of Korea's obesity drug market flipped within a year.

    Efpeglenatide, the first Korean-made obesity drug, also started being prescribed this week.

    The numbers are already in. The knee is still a hypothesis. We look at them separately.

    From the stock chart: market numbers
    01

    Wegovy launched in Korea in October 2024 and Mounjaro in August 2025.

    02

    Data from IQVIA put first-half 2026 sales at about KRW 744bn for Mounjaro and KRW 217bn for Wegovy, more than KRW 960bn combined.

    03

    By quarter: Q1 Mounjaro KRW 323.2bn vs Wegovy KRW 104bn; Q2 KRW 421.2bn vs KRW 113bn. The gap widened to about 3.7 times.

    04

    Mounjaro alone sold more than twice as much in Q2 as the cancer drug Keytruda (KRW 149.8bn). It is the No. 1 drug in Korea.

    05

    Wegovy cut its supply price by about 40% when Mounjaro arrived. Even so, Q2 sales fell 18.4% from a year earlier, though they rose 8.6% from the previous quarter, ending two straight quarters of decline.

    06

    On Oct 7, Hanmi Pharmaceutical's efpeglenatide was approved by the drug safety ministry, the 45th new drug developed in Korea and the first Korean obesity drug. Supply began on Oct 29.

    07

    Efpeglenatide's first-month price is KRW 129,800 including VAT, rising to KRW 299,200 from the fourth month. Wegovy and Mounjaro were reported at over KRW 200,000 for four weeks of the lowest dose.

    08

    In the Korean Phase 3 trial (448 people, 40-week interim results), efpeglenatide cut weight by 9.75% on average versus 0.95% for placebo; 79.4% lost at least 5% and 49.5% at least 10%.

    09

    But this is a 40-week interim analysis of a 64-week trial, while Wegovy and Mounjaro data are from 68–72 weeks, and there is no head-to-head trial. Current data cannot rank the three drugs.

    10

    On Oct 12, Novo Nordisk applied for insurance coverage of Wegovy. At the Oct 28 parliamentary audit, Health Minister Jeong Eun-kyeong said the government is reviewing putting obesity drugs such as Wegovy and Mounjaro under 'managed coverage'.

    11

    Eligibility, the obesity threshold and the co-pay have not been set. The coming question is not price but who pays how much.

    From the patient chart: the knee
    12

    The most common advice for knee arthritis patients is 'lose some weight'.

    13

    More than 3 million people a year are treated for knee arthritis (2024, NHIS data). Total knee replacements reached 78,300 in 2025, up 5.2% from 2021.

    14

    More than 80% of artificial knee joints used are imported: 82.6% in 2025.

    15

    That is why the STEP 9 trial, published in NEJM in October 2024, drew attention. It tested whether an obesity drug also helps knee pain.

    16

    It enrolled 407 people with a BMI of 30 or more, moderate knee osteoarthritis and at least moderate pain, randomized 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks.

    17

    Weight fell 13.7% versus 3.2%, and pain scores (WOMAC, 0–100) fell 41.7 points versus 27.5. Physical function scores improved about twice as much.

    18

    Three cautions in reading the numbers.

    19

    First, pain also fell 27.5 points in the placebo group. Diet and exercise counseling alone had a big effect; the drug added about 14 points.

    20

    Second, the average BMI was 40.3, 81.6% were women and the average age was 56. That is a different group from the typical knee patient in a Korean outpatient clinic.

    21

    Third, 6.7% stopped treatment due to side effects, more than double the 3.0% on placebo, mostly gastrointestinal. There is no long-term data yet.

    22

    An earlier trial of liraglutide 3.0 mg, from the same drug class, showed no difference in pain. Not every drug in this class is the same.

    23

    Semaglutide is approved for obesity and diabetes, not as an osteoarthritis treatment.

    24

    In March, Cell Metabolism published a mouse study showing changes in cartilage cell metabolism even without weight loss. In an exploratory study of 20 people, MRI after 24 weeks showed medial knee cartilage about 17% thicker on average.

    25

    The researchers themselves urged caution given the small size. It is a hypothesis.

    Overlaying the two charts
    26

    There is one question: will obesity drugs reduce knee surgery?

    27

    The numbers cannot answer it yet. All we can confirm is that the five-year trend in knee replacements is still rising after obesity drugs arrived.

    28

    The hypotheses cut both ways. Less pain before surgery could delay operations. Or severely obese patients who could not be operated on because of their weight could become candidates.

    29

    Either way, with more than 80% of artificial joints imported, foreign manufacturers will feel any change in demand first.

    30

    The cost structures also differ. Obesity drugs are now uninsured and cost hundreds of thousands of won a month, while surgery is covered by insurance. If the drugs are covered, the question becomes whether the insurance fund can afford it.

    31

    One thing is clear. When drugs take weight off, muscle tends to go too. Experts recommend weight management together with thigh-strengthening exercise for the knees.

    What to watch

    1. Results of Wegovy's coverage review and the criteria for managed coverage (BMI, comorbidities)
    2. How much share efpeglenatide takes in the Q3 IQVIA data
    3. Whether any obesity drug trial is run in Korean knee arthritis patients
    4. How weight and pain change after stopping the drug
    5. Changes in the number of knee replacements after obesity drugs launched
    In one line

    The obesity drug market is visible in numbers; the knee market is still a hypothesis. Telling numbers from hypotheses is how to read two charts.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which sells more in Korea, Mounjaro or Wegovy?

    By IQVIA data for Q2 2026, Mounjaro sold about KRW 421.2bn and Wegovy about KRW 113bn, so Mounjaro sold about 3.7 times as much.

    Do obesity drugs help knee osteoarthritis?

    In the STEP 9 trial of semaglutide, weight and knee pain fell more than with placebo. But pain also fell a lot on placebo, participants were severely obese, and the drug is not approved for osteoarthritis.

    Will obesity drugs reduce knee replacements?

    We don't know yet. Surgery could be delayed, or more patients could become eligible. It is still a hypothesis.
