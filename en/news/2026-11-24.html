    Tue, Nov 24 brief

    A day of turf fights at the National Assembly — 9 things to know today

    Home / News / Tue, Nov 24
    Today in one line

    X-rays for Korean-medicine doctors, off-site work for medical technicians, cross-hiring of pharmacists: three bills on the same day, all about who may do what.

    1. 01Policy

      Bill on X-rays for Korean-medicine doctors goes to subcommittee as both sides rally

      The welfare committee's subcommittee reviewed a medical law amendment on Nov 24 that would make any clinic founder the safety officer for diagnostic X-ray equipment. The Korean Medical Association rallied outside the Assembly calling it a back door for X-rays in Korean-medicine clinics; the Korean-medicine association held its own event urging passage.

      Dr. JordanIt is written as a safety rule, but the fight is over who may use diagnostic equipment. The outcome could also move the imaging device market.

      Source Bosa · MTN
    2. 02Policy

      Nurses back the X-ray bill; Seoul physicians demand an apology

      The Korea Nurses Association backed the amendment, citing patient choice and the burden of moving between clinics. The Seoul Medical Association said on Nov 24 that the nurses' group was undermining the licensing system and demanded an apology.

      Source Bosa
    3. 03Policy

      Medical technician bill clears subcommittee, allowing off-site work with a prescription

      The subcommittee passed an amendment to the medical technicians act on Nov 24, based on a compromise among professional groups. Technicians are defined as working 'under a doctor's direction', and off-site work is allowed on a doctor's prescription where other laws provide for it, with doctors confirming it through digital means.

      Dr. JordanThis could open the door to home visits by physical therapists and radiographers. Responsibility for prescribing and checking stays with the doctor, so payment design is the next hurdle.

      Source Bosa
    4. 04Pharmacy

      Government urges caution on bill banning pharmacist–herbal pharmacist cross-hiring

      The health ministry said a pharmacy bill barring pharmacy owners from hiring the other license type could overly restrict freedom of occupation, and urged caution. The bill was expected to struggle at the Nov 24 subcommittee.

      Source Dailypharm
    5. 05Policy

      NHIS investigator bill stalls again on Nov 18

      After deferring a vote on Nov 16, the Legislation Committee again failed to act on Nov 18 as opposition members stayed away. The NHIS says the powers would cut investigations from 11 months to three and save about KRW 200bn a year; the justice ministry says conflict-of-interest concerns remain.

      Dr. JordanThe NHIS leans on a recovery rate below 9%; the medical side leans on low referral-for-prosecution rates. Same problem, different numbers.

      Source Seoul Economic Daily · Dailymedi
    6. 06Coverage

      Co-pay for rare and severe intractable diseases falls to 7% on Dec 1

      About 1.36 million registered patients with rare or severe intractable diseases will pay 7% instead of 10% for care from Dec 1. Average annual out-of-pocket costs are expected to fall from about KRW 750,000 to KRW 530,000, with a further cut to 5% in the first half of 2028.

      Source Korea.kr
    7. 07Pharma & bio

      Barofarm buys insolvent Jungwoo Pharm for KRW 10bn

      Barofarm, a pharmacy ordering platform that listed on KOSDAQ this month, was picked as the final buyer of Jungwoo Pharm, which is in court receivership. It will pay about KRW 10bn for 100% and gain an Asan plant and about 90 products.

      Dr. JordanA distribution platform is buying a factory to make its own products. When platforms move into manufacturing, the competitive map for drugmakers changes.

      Source Dailypharm
    8. 08Devices & AI

      In the US, prescriptions can now be written and sent inside ChatGPT

      US prescribing platform Photon launched a ChatGPT plug-in. Eligible US clinicians can review treatment options, then write and send prescriptions in the same chat, with interaction checks and pharmacy price comparisons.

      Dr. JordanA sign that generative AI is moving from decision support to doing clinical work. In Korea, e-prescription rules would have to change first.

      Source Medigate News
    9. 09Regulation

      Drug safety chief inspects parametric release for IV fluids

      MFDS chief Oh Yu-kyoung visited HK inno.N's Osong plant on Nov 24 to review release based on process parameters instead of end-product sterility tests, and met three IV fluid makers. Stricter GMP had raised sterility test samples from one to ten or more.

      Source Dailypharm

    What to watch this week

    1. Outcome of the subcommittee review of the X-ray bill MTN
    2. Nov 25 Assembly forum on warehouse pharmacies and convenience-store medicines Dailypharm
    3. When the Legislation Committee takes up the NHIS investigator bill again
    4. Dec 1 start of the 7% co-pay for rare and intractable diseases
