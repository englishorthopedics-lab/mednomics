    Mon, Nov 16 brief

    Investigator bill pushed to the 18th, lab testing heads to court — 8 things to know today

    Home / News / Mon, Nov 16
    Today in one line

    All eyes are on the Legislation Committee meeting on the 18th. Whether the bill passes matters as much as how narrowly the law defines the investigators' scope.

    1. 01Policy

      NHIS special investigator bill held at the Legislation Committee; vote expected on the 18th

      The committee took up the bill on Nov 16 but did not vote, choosing to review it with other bills amending the same law. Members asked how investigations would start and raised due-process concerns about adding criminal powers to existing audit powers.

      Dr. JordanInvestigations would be limited to clinics run by non-doctors and rented-license pharmacies. What worries clinics is concentrating audit and criminal powers in one agency.

      Source Bosa · Khanews · Dailypharm
    2. 02Policy

      Doctors rally at the Assembly; NHIS offers to limit scope to illegally opened clinics

      The Korean Medical Association rallied outside the Assembly on Nov 16 demanding the bill be withdrawn. The NHIS said it would limit investigations to non-doctor-owned clinics and rented-license pharmacies, not ordinary care or billing, and proposed an external review board.

      Source Dailypharm · Nurse News
    3. 03Clinics

      Hypertension society threatens legal action over lab-testing fee overhaul

      At its autumn meeting in Gwangju on Nov 15, the society warned that cuts to lab-testing fees would hurt internal medicine clinics treating chronic disease. It threatened legal action against the notice and also opposed the NHIS investigator plan.

      Dr. JordanLab tests are a large part of neighborhood clinic revenue. The question is whether fee changes will reshape how chronic disease is managed.

      Source Bosa
    4. 04Devices & AI

      AI scribe Neardoc reaches 500 hospitals in five months

      The AI scribe drafts a SOAP note from the consultation in about five seconds. It says it has reached 500 hospitals and 970,000 records in five months, deletes raw audio immediately and checks each sentence against the conversation.

      Dr. JordanWhether saved charting time goes to patients or to more visits is the hospital's choice.

      Source Bosa
    5. 05Pharma & bio

      Three Chinese cancer drugs: Europe says yes, the US says no

      Europe's CHMP recommended approval of lung cancer drugs aumolertinib and sintilimab, while the US FDA did not approve ivonescimab. Regulators split on drugs from the same country.

      Source Bosa
    6. 06Research

      Oral combination shows promise in endometrial cancer after immunotherapy

      In an early trial across 12 sites in Korea, the US and Japan, E7386 plus lenvatinib shrank tumors by the set threshold in 36.7% of patients and controlled disease in 70%. Among patients new to lenvatinib, 57.1% saw tumors shrink.

      Source Bosa
    7. 07Conferences

      KDDW 2026 in Seoul, Nov 19–21, with 2,000 attendees from 30 countries

      Eight gastroenterology societies will present 456 studies. Updated Korean guidelines for MASLD and inflammatory bowel disease are due.

      Source Bosa
    8. 08Regulation

      MFDS holds Innovative Medical Device Policy Forum 2026

      The drug safety ministry hosted a forum on Nov 16 for companies with or seeking innovative device status, joined by HIRA, on faster market entry.

      Source Bosa

    What to watch this week

    1. Whether the Legislation Committee passes the NHIS investigator bill on Nov 18
    2. Follow-up to the Ajou University pediatric ER raid and the urgent appeal to UN human rights bodies (Nov 11) Docdocdoc
    3. Legal challenges by societies and physician groups over the lab-testing notice
    4. Guideline changes for MASLD and IBD at KDDW 2026 (Nov 19–21)
